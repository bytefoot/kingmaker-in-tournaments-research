# P2 — Bradley–Terry is not organiser-proof

**Status:** example re-verified exactly; the characterisation (which $k$-th strongest can be made favourite for $n=4$) is
not attempted yet.

## Statement
Under BT there exist strengths $\gamma$ and a bracket in which a player other than the strongest is the unique favourite.

## Example (re-verified)
$\gamma = (1.0,\ 0.95,\ 0.8,\ -3.0)$ for A, B, C, D; $P_{ij} = 1/(1+e^{\gamma_j-\gamma_i})$.
Computed by `theory/verify_examples.py` from exact sympy expressions, evaluated to 30 digits (each bracket's $W$ sums to 1 to 30 digits).

| Bracket | $W_A$ | $W_B$ | $W_C$ | $W_D$ | Favourite |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| {A,B} \| {C,D} | 0.286635 | 0.266731 | **0.446231** | 0.000403 | C (3rd strongest) |
| {A,C} \| {B,D} | 0.286665 | **0.500344** | 0.212618 | 0.000373 | B (2nd strongest) |
| {A,D} \| {B,C} | **0.520240** | 0.266770 | 0.212626 | 0.000365 | A (strongest) |

So the claimed numbers hold ($W_C \approx 0.446 > W_A \approx 0.287$). This instance also shows more: **every player
except the weakest is the favourite of some bracket ($K = 3$ of 4)**. The mechanism: the three strong players are nearly
equal, D is hopeless, so whoever is drawn against D gets a free pass to the final, and that is worth more than a
0.2-logit skill gap.

## Hand check of $W_C$ in {A,B} | {C,D}
$W_C = P_{CD}\,\big(P_{AB}P_{CA} + P_{BA}P_{CB}\big)$ with $P_{CD} = \sigma(3.8)$, $P_{AB} = \sigma(0.05)$,
$P_{CA} = \sigma(-0.2)$, $P_{CB} = \sigma(-0.15)$:
$0.97812 \times (0.51250 \times 0.45017 + 0.48750 \times 0.46257) = 0.97812 \times 0.45621 = 0.44623$. ✓
