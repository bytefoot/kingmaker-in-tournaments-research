# P6 (new, found in E1) — Under BT the kingmakeable set is a top segment

**Status:** conjecture, supported by 1,200 random BT matrices at n = 8 (exhaustive) and 20 at n = 16 (local search,
so only for the players search found). No counterexample search or proof yet.

## Statement
Under BT with strengths $g_1 > g_2 > \dots > g_n$, if player $r$ is kingmakeable then so is every player $1, \dots, r-1$.
Equivalently, the kingmakeable set is $\{1, \dots, K\}$ for some $K$.

## Evidence
`outputs/csv/e1_n8_raw.csv`, rows with κ = 0 (3 scales × 2 cycle variants × 200 matrices; at κ = 0 both variants are pure BT):
in all 1,200 matrices the worst-ranked kingmakeable player has rank exactly K. Distribution of K:
1 → 642, 2 → 251, 3 → 175, 4 → 100, 5 → 29, 6 → 3. With cycles the property quickly fails: the share of matrices
whose kingmakeable set is a top segment is 0.987 at κ = 0.1, 0.948 at κ = 0.3 and 0.878 at κ = 0.6 (all 6 cells pooled).

## Relation to P3
It would follow if, for a bracket σ where $j$ is the unique favourite and $i$ is stronger than $j$, swapping $i$ and $j$
gave a bracket in which $i$ is the unique favourite. P3 gives $W_i(\sigma') \ge W_j(\sigma)$, but it also has to be shown
that no third player $k$ overtakes $i$ after the swap, so P6 does not follow from P3 directly.

## Corollary (if true)
The weakest player is kingmakeable only when K = n, i.e. never unless every player is. E1 found the BT weakest player
kingmakeable in 0 of 1,200 matrices.
