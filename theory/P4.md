# P4 — Cycles break BT swap-monotonicity

**Status:** original example re-verified exactly. Cleaner round-number example found and verified exactly (below).
Minimum-κ search done (`theory/p4_search.py`, `outputs/csv/p4_grid.csv`, `outputs/csv/p4_min_kappa.csv`). At n = 4 the
"max Borda rank crowned" question is the same as "can the weakest be crowned", since rank 4 is the maximum.

## Example (re-verified)
Row beats column:

|   | A | B | C | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | – | 0.95 | 0.20 | 0.62 |
| B | 0.05 | – | 0.72 | 0.50 |
| C | 0.80 | 0.28 | – | 0.39 |
| D | 0.38 | 0.50 | 0.61 | – |

Mean win rate vs the others (Borda): A = 59/100, **B = 127/300 ≈ 0.4233 (lowest)**, C = 49/100, D = 149/300.

Exact win probabilities (`theory/verify_examples.py`, `fractions.Fraction`; every row sums to exactly 1):

| Bracket | $W_A$ | $W_B$ | $W_C$ | $W_D$ | Favourite |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| {A,B} \| {C,D} | 43339/100000 = 0.4334 | 0.0293 | 0.3019 | 0.2355 | A |
| {A,C} \| {B,D} | 157/1000 = 0.1570 | **293/1000 = 0.2930** | 67/250 = 0.2680 | 141/500 = 0.2820 | **B** |
| {A,D} \| {B,C} | 0.4588 | 0.1591 | 0.1804 | 0.2017 | A |

The claim holds: the player with the lowest average win rate is the unique favourite of bracket {A,C} | {B,D}, with
$W_B = 293/1000$ exactly.

## Hand check of $W_B$ in {A,C} | {B,D}
$W_B = P_{BD}\,(P_{AC}P_{BA} + P_{CA}P_{BC}) = 0.5\,(0.2 \cdot 0.05 + 0.8 \cdot 0.72) = 0.5 \times 0.586 = 0.293$. ✓

Why it works: B's only bad matchup is A, and A is likely to be knocked out by its own nemesis C in the semi; B
beats C comfortably. That combination is impossible under BT: if C beats A 80% of the time and A beats B 95% of the
time, BT forces C to beat B with probability above 95%, yet here B beats C 72% of the time (an A→B→C→A cycle).

## Cleaner example (one-decimal probabilities; used for F1)
Players relabelled so A is strongest and D weakest by both strength score s and Borda (row beats column):

|   | A | B | C | D | Borda | s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | – | 0.7 | 0.3 | 0.9 | 0.633 | 0.549 |
| B | 0.3 | – | 0.7 | 0.4 | 0.467 | −0.101 |
| C | 0.7 | 0.3 | – | 0.4 | 0.467 | −0.101 |
| D | 0.1 | 0.6 | 0.6 | – | **0.433** | **−0.347** |

κ = 0.758. Exact win probabilities (`Fraction`, each row sums to 1):

| Bracket | $W_A$ | $W_B$ | $W_C$ | $W_D$ | Favourite |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| {A,B} \| {C,D} | 231/500 = 0.462 | 0.156 | 0.232 | 0.150 | A |
| {A,C} \| {B,D} | 123/500 = 0.246 | 29/125 = 0.232 | 63/250 = 0.252 | **27/100 = 0.270** | **D** |
| {A,D} \| {B,C} | 0.522 | 0.217 | 0.201 | 0.060 | A |

Hand check: $W_D = P_{DB}\,(P_{AC}P_{DA} + P_{CA}P_{DC}) = 0.6\,(0.3\cdot0.1 + 0.7\cdot0.6) = 0.6 \times 0.45 = 0.27$. ✓
Mechanism: D's only bad matchup is A (10%), A's nemesis C removes A 70% of the time, and D beats B and C 60% of the time.
The margin is small (0.270 vs 0.252): this bracket makes all four players nearly equal, and D comes out just ahead.

## How much cyclicity is needed? (n = 4)
- **Round-number grid** {0.1, …, 0.9}⁶ (531,441 matrices): the weakest player (unique under both s and Borda) can be
  crowned in 1,584 matrices. The smallest κ is 0.755 (margin 0.005). The κ–margin Pareto front runs from (0.755, 0.005)
  through (0.758, 0.018) (the example above) to (0.971, 0.114). The coarse {0.1, 0.3, …, 0.9} grid has no example that is
  unambiguous under both definitions of "weakest".
- **Continuous search** (400,000 random + 3,000 hill-climb steps; all P in [0.05, 0.95]; same unique weakest under both
  definitions; margin > 10⁻³): feasible at **κ = 0.559**, which is an upper bound on the true minimum, not a proved minimum.
  Without the probability bound and the same-weakest requirement, the search reached κ ≈ 0.25, but the solution it found
  uses near-certain matches (P ≈ 0 or 1), and its s-weakest and Borda-weakest players differ.
- So at n = 4 our searches found **no** non-degenerate example below κ ≈ 0.56. This is evidence, not a proof of a lower
  bound: it fits E1 (n = 8: weakest crowned only from κ ≈ 0.6–0.8), and a true threshold κ* remains open (P5).
